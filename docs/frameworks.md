# Framework Areas

MindShift integrates established lenses on learning, systems, and cognition. None
is treated as authoritative on its own; each is a tool applied where it has
leverage. The value is in how they combine within the core runtime.

> **These frameworks are instruments, not substance.** The substance is the
> [thesis](thesis.md): a process that improves itself. Every lens below is
> optional vocabulary for running or sharpening that loop — remove them all and
> the thesis is unharmed. None may become load-bearing; promoting any one of them
> to "essential" would contradict the very claim it is meant to serve.

## Boundary Note — Frameworks Are Child Artifacts

Frameworks are not the final output of MindShift. Frameworks are child artifacts
produced by abstraction discovery. The parent process is:

```text
Reality → Pattern → Abstraction → Transfer
```

A framework belongs here only when it helps an observed pattern become a
transferable abstraction that improves future cognition.

## First Principles Thinking

Decompose a problem to its fundamental truths and reason up from there, rather
than reasoning by analogy from existing solutions.

## Pattern Recognition

Detect recurring structures, invariants, and relationships across observations.
The substrate of understanding.

## Learning Systems

Treat any learner — individual, team, organization, or machine — as a system that
takes in signal and updates behavior.

## Meta-Learning

Learning about learning. Improving the process that produces understanding. The
project's highest-leverage area.

## Reflexive Intelligence

Intelligence that observes and modifies its own operation. The point at which a
learning loop turns back on itself.

## Bateson Levels of Learning

Gregory Bateson's hierarchy — from simple correction (Learning I), to learning to
learn (Learning II), to transforming the premises of learning (Learning III).
Used to locate *what level* of learning a situation involves.

## Dilts Logical Levels

Robert Dilts' nested levels — environment, behavior, capability, beliefs/values,
identity, purpose. Used to find the level at which a change will actually take
hold.

## Cybernetics

The study of control and communication through feedback. The formal grounding for
treating learning as a feedback-regulated process.

## Kaizen / PDCA

Continuous, incremental improvement via Plan → Do → Check → Act. A disciplined
expression of the core runtime at small scale.

## OODA Loops

Observe → Orient → Decide → Act (John Boyd). Emphasizes tempo: the speed and
quality of the loop is itself a decisive advantage.

## Systems Thinking

Reason about wholes, relationships, stocks, flows, delays, and feedback rather
than isolated parts. Reveals where leverage and unintended consequences live.

## Flow Theory

The conditions (clear goals, immediate feedback, balanced challenge) under which
sustained, high-quality engagement and learning occur.

## Feedback Systems

The general design concern: making the signal between action and result fast,
honest, and legible.

## Cognitive Architecture

How perception, memory, modeling, and action are organized into a system capable
of learning and adapting.

## Recursive Improvement

The overarching aim: every cycle improves future cycles, including the cycle that
does the improving.
